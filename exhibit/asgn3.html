Contest Only!

Modifications I made for the contest only. I added 3 additional voxel types. So, in order, the voxel types are air, dirt, stone, moss, ore. The priority is that moss and dirt are on the outside, and stone and ore on the inside of structures. I upload them to the GPU in the form of a 3D texture, then in the fragment shader I sample the voxel grid to determine the texture to use. This keeps my vertex data super lean and minimal. Also I can use up to 255 different voxel types. This is because that is the format of the 3D texture I use, and the actual 2D texture data is stored as a texture atlas meaning I can use as many as I want as long as the texture is not too large and the 3D texture can actually index it.

I made the boids small so they look like dust particles, or maybe bugs? I like them being boids but I could make them more like dust.

Original Info

The three place modes are place block, where left click places a block and right click removes a block. Cast Ray which places a line of blocks 100 blocks long (doesn't stop at filled blocks). And Shoot Lazer, which clears a 3 block radius around a ray, and pushes boids out of the way.

So this was the most work I've done for one of these assignments. The world is infinite in 3 directions and uses 3D value noise based on the noise chapter of the Book of Shaders, although the core noise algorithm is just some xors and multiplies I threw together, basically just xors and multiplying by a prime.

The world is generated on demand, in a 2x2x2 chunk region, with each chunk being 32x32x32. Chunks are marked dirty when modified, all other chunks will be deleted when they are further than 2 chunks from the camera.

I also implemented the Digital Differential Analyzer which allows for O(n) efficient voxel raycasts. Its pretty neat, you essentially do some easy precalculation and then you just do comparisons and additions. This allows for large reach and also the ray and lazer place modes.

In addition I added a simple boid simulation on top. The boids don't really interact with the voxel grid because I ran out of time and effort. The ideal way to do this would be to have each boid do a raycast to check for collisions and turn out of the way, but I have a super janky version which samples the underlying value noise and tries to avoid areas where the noise values are too close to the threshhold. The boid movement is noticably chunky, this is because I only update boids on every other frame in order to allow for slightly more boids. It kind of worked, although because I don't use any spatial data structures for the boids the number of boids is still fairly low, and is the majority of the frame time.

The lazer mode will clear out a large region of voxels in a ray, as well as push boids in that ray away as if there were an invisible push, because I think it was cool.
